Otto · product exploration · October 2026

Two eyes, living in the notch.

The O’s in Otto’s logo already have eyes: holes that drift and blink in the footer of the site. This page explores how that same creature could live inside the product, with four directions from “almost invisible” to “always there”. Every stage below is live: pick a moment, and move your cursor over it.

01 · Vocabulary

Nine expressions, two circles.

Each eye is the O from the logo: a white disc with the black hole as pupil. Everything else is made with three things: where the pupil looks, a lid that comes down (and can tilt), and a lower lid that pushes up into a smile. No mouth, no brows, no extra shapes. ● reads well at notch size (10–14 px), ○ only works large.

Hover a tile: the eyes follow your cursor, with the inertia of the footer logo.

02 · Direction A

The detail.

The eyes are almost never there. They show up for half a second at the moments that deserve a reaction (the notch opening, a to-do done, an empty day) and then they’re gone. Otto stays a tool; the eyes are a signature, like a wink in the corner of a page.

From the references
Espacio La Nube’s restraint: one small thing moving, lots of quiet around it. The footer logo’s blink, reused once instead of always.
Motion character
Minimal and precise. Short ease-out fades, a single blink, no overshoot.

SpecValue
Appear / disappearopacity 0→1 in 160 ms ease-out, hold 600–900 ms, out in 240 ms
Blinklid down 70 ms, up 110 ms (ease-in / ease-out). One blink per appearance, never more.
Lookcritically damped spring, response 0.25 s, no overshoot
With the notchappear after the panel has settled (+ 320 ms), never during the open spring

Strong

  • Can’t get annoying: it’s rare by design.
  • Zero cost to reading: nothing sits on top of information.
  • Cheapest to build.

Risky

  • Too rare to become “a character”; many people never notice it.
  • Doesn’t carry any information, so it’s pure decoration.
03 · Direction B

The tell.

The eyes live in the closed notch, in the left wing where the meeting icon sits today, and they are a status light with a face. Calm and half-lidded when nothing is due, looking at the countdown when a meeting is close, wide when you’re late, pleased when you tick something off. You read the state of your day before you open anything.

From the references
CoolDock’s idea of a system utility with a wink that stays clean. FacilPay’s confidence: one bold element doing a clear job.
Motion character
Soft and organic. Springs with a little life, slow breathing at rest.

SpecValue
Idlelids at 35 %, breathing scale 1 → 1.03 over 4 s sine; a glance every 8–14 s; a blink every 4–7 s
Lookspring response 0.45 s, damping 0.72 (≈ 6 % overshoot). Pupils lead, lids follow 60 ms later.
State changelids/tilt spring response 0.35 s, damping 0.8; never two expressions within 1.2 s
With the notchon open, the eyes stay in the wing for the first 120 ms, then drift to the panel’s top-left corner (matched geometry) and shrink to 10 px
Sizeclosed: 11 px per eye, 5 px gap; never larger than the wing’s height minus 10

Strong

  • Every animation means something: the eyes are information.
  • Replaces the meeting dot with something more legible from the corner of your eye.
  • Feels like the same creature as the footer (slow drift, blinks).

Risky

  • Always-visible movement in the menu bar can pull focus: idle motion must stay below notice.
  • Needs a clear rule for which state wins (meeting vs. open to-dos).
04 · Direction C

The gesture.

The eyes are the opening itself. Asleep and invisible in the closed notch; on hover they open, look down, and the panel unfolds from their gaze. Once it’s open, they shrink into the bar and keep pointing at what matters, then close again as the notch closes. One continuous creature, no popping in and out.

From the references
Supaste’s continuity, where an element turns into the next section instead of disappearing. FacilPay’s staged reveal, here as an opening in three beats: wake, look, unfold.
Motion character
Playful and snappy. Anticipation before the open, a clear overshoot, then still.

SpecValue
Wake (hover)lids 1 → 0 in 180 ms, pupils dip −4 px (anticipation, 80 ms), then look down
Unfoldnotch opens 140 ms after the look; spring response 0.38 s, damping 0.62 (≈ 12 % overshoot), eyes ride the bottom edge
Settleeyes travel to the bar’s centre and shrink 18 → 9 px, 420 ms; from there they look at the focused row
Closereverse path, lids close as the notch reaches its closed size; 360 ms total

Strong

  • Gives the gesture of opening the notch a signature nobody else has.
  • Personality without adding anything to the open panel.
  • The most memorable in a demo or a video.

Risky

  • The open is the thing you do 50 times a day: any extra beat must stay under ~150 ms or it feels slow.
  • Hardest to tune with the existing open spring.
05 · Direction D

The companion.

Otto is a little someone who lives in your notch. The eyes are always there, a size up, following your cursor around the screen, reacting to everything: they look at a meeting when it’s due, squint with pleasure at a done to-do, get sleepy at the end of the day, and take the whole panel on an empty one.

From the references
The footer logo at full volume: eyes that track the pointer everywhere. CoolDock’s playfulness pushed further.
Motion character
Soft and organic, with the most freedom: gaze follows the cursor with weight, saccades, idle fidgets.

SpecValue
Cursor trackingspring response 0.5 s, damping 0.85; dead zone of 40 px so small mouse moves don’t twitch
Saccadeswhen the cursor is still for 3 s: random glance, 90 ms jump, hold 0.6–1.4 s
Reactionssquash on surprise scaleY 1.15, back with damping 0.55; happy squint 600 ms
Sizeclosed: 14 px per eye, in a widened notch (+44 px); open: avatar in the panel’s top-left at 18 px

Strong

  • Instantly lovable; the most “Otto” of the four.
  • Great in marketing and onboarding.

Risky

  • Constant motion at the top of the screen, all day: very likely to tire, especially when screen-sharing or presenting.
  • Needs a global mouse monitor (permission, battery).
  • Closest to “mascot” and furthest from a quiet tool.

Recommendation: B, with one beat of C.

Make the eyes the status of the closed notch (B): they replace the meeting dot, carry real information and are the same calm creature as the footer. Then borrow a single moment from C: on hover the lids lift before the panel opens, kept under 120 ms so it never slows you down. Skip cursor tracking for now; it’s the part that tires and costs the most.

Rules to keep it quiet: idle motion only every 8–14 s, never two expressions within 1.2 s, everything stops while screen sharing and under Reduce Motion.

Building it in SwiftUIEffort
The eyes themselvesEasy. Two Circles, a pupil offset, lids as Rectangle/Ellipse masked by the eye. Animate with .spring(response:dampingFraction:). One OttoEyes(expression:look:) view.
Expressions and blinksEasy. An enum of expressions mapped to lid/tilt/pupil values; blinks with PhaseAnimator or a timer (macOS 14+, fallback with withAnimation on 13).
Status in the closed notch (B)Easy–medium. Reuses the meeting wing; the work is the state priority rules in NotchController.
Eyes moving from closed to open (B, C)Medium. matchedGeometryEffect between the wing and the panel; must be tuned with the existing open spring.
Wake before open (C)Medium. Needs a short pre-open phase in the hover → open sequence.
Cursor tracking (D)Costly. NSEvent.addGlobalMonitorForEvents(.mouseMoved), throttled; continuous redraws at the top of the screen; extra battery and a privacy question.
Breathing / idleEasy, but watch energy. Use TimelineView(.animation(minimumInterval: 1/20)) only while visible; stop when the display sleeps.
Prototype for discussion. The references (Espacio La Nube, FacilPay, Supaste, CoolDock) weren’t reachable from this machine; the directions draw on the Otto footer and on memory of those sites.
